II. Introduction
In recent years, the popularity of drones (also known as unmanned aerial vehicles, UAVs) in commercial markets and civil applications has significantly increased [1]-[8]. However, the misuse of drones has caused severe threats to public safety and personal privacy. For example, in January 2015, a small drone crashed on the White House lawn; in October 2021, two drones illegally monitored the French President's residence. To counter these threats, geographic fences and drone prohibition zones need to be established in sensitive areas such as airports, nuclear facilities, hospitals, etc., to prevent the invasion of registered drones. However, there are still many unregistered drones entering these areas, making the identification of malicious drones very urgent.
Passive radio frequency (RF) methods have broad prospects in the field of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) identification due to their strong adaptability to urban environments and immunity from non-line-of-sight (NLoS) scenarios. As shown in Figure 1, taking DJI Phantom 4 Pro as an example, communication signals between the drone and the ground controller include flight control signals (FCS) and video transmission signals (VTS). Specifically, the flight control signals carry flight instructions and flight logs (such as speed and altitude), while the video transmission signals carry the video stream captured by the drone's onboard camera. By deploying RF receivers to collect RF signals from the surrounding environment, it is possible to effectively monitor communication activities of intruding UAVs.
Existing research work on radio frequency-based applications [9]-[21] primarily focuses on supervised scenarios, where methods are based on the assumption of a closed set, meaning that the category of the test signal must be consistent with the category of the training signals. However, as the number of drones surges, it is unrealistic to exhaust all possible drone categories within a closed set. Consequently, Open Set Recognition (OSR) for drones has become an urgent issue to address. OSR requires consideration of scenarios where unknown samples not present during the training phase appear during the testing phase, necessitating that the classifier can accurately classify known samples while also effectively handling unknown ones [22]-[23].
Currently, research on the open-set recognition of unmanned aerial vehicles based on radio frequency is not widely conducted. Compared with common open-set recognition methods, its challenges mainly lie in the following three aspects:
1  
Effective identification of drones with the same brand: Existing methods for open set recognition typically employ Convolutional Features Extractor (CFE), which perform well in tasks requiring strong local feature correlation, such as visual images. However, due to the high similarity in signal modulation among drones of the same brand, local features become difficult to distinguish. Consequently, CFEs are ill-suited for identifying drones of the same brand. There is an urgent need to develop specialized feature extractors specifically for drones.
2  
Unknown UAV Fine Segmentation: Unlike open-set recognition commonly used for images, texts, and speeches that can be reclassified by humans, the fine segmentation of unknown UAVs based on radio frequency (RF) requires not only detecting unknown signals but also classifying multiple unknown signals into their accurate categories.
3  
Insufficient Prior Knowledge of Unknown UAVs: Some existing open-set recognition methods require prior knowledge of unknown categories to determine optimal boundary thresholds (rather than human-defined thresholds), aiming to enhance recognition performance. However, in real scenarios, it is often difficult to obtain prior knowledge of unknown categories, thus there is an urgent need to explore adaptive thresholds that rely solely on known category knowledge.
To address these challenges, this paper proposes a signal semantics-based RF UAV open-set recognition (S3R) method, which consists of three core modules: feature generation, semantic construction, and semantic classification. In the feature generation module, Short Time Fourier Transform (STFT) is employed to generate time-frequency spectra (TFS), achieving decoupling between target UAV signals and other interfering signals within the same frequency band; In the semantic construction module, texture extractors (TE) and position extractors (PE) learn texture features (bandwidth, temporal span, etc.) and position features (interval, frequency points, etc.) from the time-frequency spectrum respectively, reducing overlap of different semantics and enhancing the cohesion of similar semantics through structural optimization; In the semantic classification module, a two-stage semantic classification method is designed: the first stage acquires adaptive thresholds by analyzing outliers in closed sets with known semantics, using them as classification boundaries to detect unknown semantics; The second stage refines the identification of unknown semantics through clustering, and introduces cluster quality analysis to estimate the most probable number of unknown categories.

Through this transformation, it effectively decouples the flight control signal (FCS) and video transmission signal (VTS) of the target drone from the interference signals in the same frequency band, providing clear time-frequency domain representations for subsequent feature extraction.
(II) The Semantic Constructing Module Designs the Texture Extractor (TE) and the Position Extractor (PE), respectively, to extract two types of key features from the time-frequency spectrum.
•  
Texture Extractor: Constructed based on the Dilation Convolutional Layer (DCL), it enhances the joint perception capability of bandwidth, temporal span, and other texture features for flight control signals and video transmission signals through providing multi-level receptive fields. It aids in distinguishing signals from different brands and models of drones.
•  
Position Extractor: Designed based on the Transformer architecture, focusing on capturing the positional features of flight control signals and video transmission signals in the time-frequency domain, such as intervals and frequency points. It solves the recognition difficulty caused by modulation similarity among drone signals of the same brand.
The subsequent steps involve the integration of texture and positional features, as well as the optimization of signal semantics through reducing overlap between different semantic categories and enhancing the aggregation of similar semantics, thereby enhancing the category discrimination capability.
(III) The Semantic Classification Module adopts a two-stage semantic classification strategy:
• 
In the first stage, a semantic classifier based on outlier analysis is proposed. By searching for outliers within each known category in the closed set as boundary thresholds, it can adaptively detect unknown samples without prior knowledge of unknown classes.
• 
In the second stage, an enhancement of the original semantics is introduced by the basic features from the intermediate layer of the texture extractor. This expands the semantic space to sparsely represent unknown semantics, followed by fine classification of detected unknown samples through clustering. Additionally, cluster quality analysis is used to estimate the most probable number of unknown classes.

The basic features from the intermediate layer of the extractor are fused to enhance the signal semantics, and then, through clustering, the fine recognition of unknown samples is achieved.
